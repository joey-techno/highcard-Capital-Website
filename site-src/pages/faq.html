The products.

Which product is right for me: Term Loan, SBA, HELOC, or Line of Credit?

The one-line version: a defined purchase wants a Term Loan; the lowest possible payment on a big move wants SBA; home equity you’d like working instead of sitting wants a HELOC; recurring cash-flow swings want a Line of Credit.

The honest version: you don’t have to be right. The comparison table gets you close, and your advisor prices the plausible fits side by side. One application covers both a Term Loan and a Line of Credit anyway.

Do I qualify?

Most funded merchants check three boxes: six-plus months in business, roughly $8K or more in monthly revenue, and a U.S. business bank account. Credit helps but doesn’t rule: files from the 500s get funded when deposits are steady.

SBA holds a higher bar: typically two-plus years in business and credit in the high 600s. HELOC adds home equity and documentable income. If you’re under a bar today, your advisor will tell you exactly which one and what changes the answer.

My credit isn’t perfect. Is this a waste of time?

No. For most of our products, twelve months of healthy deposits outweigh a dented score, since lenders in our network read revenue first. And because checking your options is a soft pull, finding out costs your score nothing.

If another lender turned you down, we dig into why and build a plan to get you back on track. Sometimes that’s funding today; sometimes it’s a ninety-day path to a much better offer. Either way you leave the call knowing where you stand.

I already have a loan or an advance. Can you still help?

Usually, yes: this is one of the most common files we see. Depending on the numbers, that’s a consolidation that replaces expensive daily-payment debt with something saner, or a second position that layers carefully on top of what exists.

One warning we give freely: piling short-term advances on top of each other is how good businesses drown. Bring your current statements and your advisor will map the exits, including the ones that don’t involve us.

Applying.

How fast can I actually get funded?

Term Loans and Lines of Credit: options usually within 24–48 hours of a complete application, funding in as little as 24 hours after you accept. Most files fund within a few business days. HELOC: three to four weeks; appraisals set the pace. SBA: two to ten weeks depending on program and lender.

Anyone quoting SBA in “days” is selling you something. You’ll get a real calendar for your specific file on your first advisor call.

Will checking my options hurt my credit?

No. Seeing your options is a soft pull, invisible to other lenders and harmless to your score. A hard pull happens only if you accept an offer, and we tell you before it does. That sequence never changes, on any product.

What documents do I need?

For Term Loans and Lines of Credit: three to six months of business bank statements plus the two-minute application. That’s genuinely it for most files. SBA adds tax returns, a P&L, and a personal financial statement (the full list; your advisor assembles the package with you). HELOC adds income documentation and property details.

What happens after I apply?

Within the hour: confirmation and a named advisor on your file. Same business day: a ten-minute call to fill in the picture. Within 24–48 hours: options in writing. Then you decide: accept, ask questions, or walk away at zero cost.

And the fear behind the question, answered plainly: your data is never sold. Applying here does not put your phone on fifty call lists. One advisor calls you, and it’s the same one every time. The full play-by-play is on How it works.

Cost & the fine print.

What does it cost to work with you?

Zero to apply, zero to see options, zero to walk away. When funding closes, the lender compensates us. That’s the business model, stated in plain sight. Some products carry lender-side costs like closing or draw fees; every one of them appears in your written cost sheet before you sign.

The rule that governs everything: your costs are in writing before your signature, every time. If a number ever surprises you after signing, we failed, and we’ve built the process so it can’t.

Are you a lender or a broker?

A brokerage, not a direct lender, and that’s on purpose. High Card Capital arranges financing through a network of lending partners rather than lending off one balance sheet. One bank can only offer its own shelf; we shop the whole table and make lenders compete for your file.

What that means practically: we’re paid by lenders at closing (so applying is free), our incentive is your file closing on terms you’ll actually accept, and when the honest answer is “take the bank’s offer, it’s better,” your advisor says exactly that. More on who we are.
